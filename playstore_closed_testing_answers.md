# FamilyLedger — Closed Testing Feedback Summary (Google Play production access)

All numbers below were pulled directly from the live Firestore database on 2026-08-19, not estimated. Suspicious/automated accounts (Google's own pre-launch-report crawler pattern — ~16 accounts with generic Western names + numeric-suffix Gmail addresses, all created in the same short burst window; plus 1 bot probe account; plus 7 stale no-email dev/seed records) were identified and excluded from every count below. The real, evidenced tester base is **55 accounts**, of which **45 were active in the last 14 days** and **28 in the last 7 days**.

---

### 1. How did you recruit testers?

Testers were recruited personally, through direct relationships rather than any paid or public channel: immediate and extended family members, close friends, a small number of colleagues, and an existing WhatsApp group used to coordinate and share the install link. Because the app's actual purpose is shared expense tracking within a family/friend circle, these same people were the natural first testers — they had a genuine reason to use it, not just to click through it.

### 2. How easy or difficult was tester recruitment?

Moderate effort — not immediate. Most people agreed to try it when asked directly, but getting them to actually install the build and use it for more than a day or two took some follow-up (reminders in the WhatsApp group, nudging specific people who hadn't opened it yet). This is reflected in the real signup pattern: the 55 real accounts joined gradually over roughly two weeks (Aug 4–19), not all at once, which matches a genuine, personally-recruited rollout rather than a bulk import.

### 3. How did testers engage with your app?

Real usage over the most recent 14-day window:

- **Games**: 261 multiplayer game sessions created across all 7 game types (193 Rummy, 29 Ludo, 14 Sweep, 8 Business, 8 Sequence, 6 Scramble, 3 Chess), 247 of them played through to completion, involving 13 distinct players in active matches.
- **Expense tracking** (the app's core feature): 302 expense/income entries logged (282 expenses, 20 income entries) by 15 distinct users, across 48 groups created in total.
- **Chat**: 75 direct messages, 73 Rummy in-game chat messages, 19 group chat messages, 10 Sweep chat messages — real back-and-forth between testers, not just one-way usage.
- **Overall activity**: 2,010 total in-app events (game invites, expense edits, group creation/joins, comments, reminders acted on, etc.) generated by 36 distinct users in 14 days.
- **Consistency across 14 days**: of the 55 real tester accounts, 45 (82%) were active at some point in the 14-day window and 28 (51%) in just the last 7 days — engagement wasn't a single burst at install time, it continued through the whole period, driven mostly by the game features (which naturally bring people back for turns/rematches) and ongoing expense-splitting within real family groups.

Collected via the app's own in-app Feedback screen — 42 submissions total (30 suggestions, 10 bug reports, 2 general feedback) from at least 7 distinctly identifiable testers. Concrete, specific examples (not generic praise):

- *"In scramble multi-player game leadership board is missing"* / *"play screen should be fixed, at the start the player has to scroll up to see the play area"* / *"For every solved word provide meaning and 1 sentence usage"* / *"provide option for quick rematch"* — preeti rajput (four separate, specific feature requests about one game mode)
- *"Recent Activities: if this is like notifications there should be a clear button, and if it's specifically recent activities we should be able to see older activities"* — Manoj Rajput
- *"Chats are not sorted, chat edit option required along with edited mark, on click on profile pic show options (view profile, view balances related to that person, chat privately etc)"* — Manoj Rajput

### 5. How did you collect feedback?

Primarily an **in-app Feedback screen** (accessible from every user's profile) where testers submit a typed message, optionally tag it as a suggestion/feedback/bug, and attach a screenshot — this fed directly into an admin review dashboard where each item could be replied to and marked resolved. Secondarily, informal feedback and nudges to actually use the app came through the **WhatsApp group** used to recruit testers.

### 6. What bugs or issues were found?

Real, specific bugs found during testing (not "no issues found"):

- Download button on an admin screen not working
- App lock (PIN) required re-entry every time the app was backgrounded and resumed, even briefly
- Activity/data lists not sorted consistently; no sorting/filtering available across the app
- Chat messages not sorted correctly; no way to edit a sent message or see an "edited" mark
- Large expense amounts not displaying correctly in some views, with unclear limits on input size
- Feed/activity items and scheduled (recurring) expenses not behaving reliably
- Admin screen: tapping a feedback screenshot forced a download instead of showing it full-size
- The in-app "About" screen showing incorrect usage statistics
- Ludo: a chosen pawn color could visually mismatch its board position after a mid-lobby color change
- Ludo: two-player games could seat players in adjacent (not opposite) board quadrants depending on which colors they picked
- Scramble (word game): certain valid dictionary words were rejected due to an overly small offline word list, and duplicate words could be regenerated within the same match with no way to solve that round
- A server-side data-storage bug (`Nested arrays are not allowed`) that silently broke starting a new multiplayer Scramble match

### 7. What changes did you make based on feedback?

Each item below was a direct response to a specific tester report, not a proactive/unprompted change:

- **v1.2.1 (versionCode 8–12)**: added native local-notification support so push notifications display correctly while the app is open; fixed invite/share links pointing at a stale domain after a custom-domain migration; fixed Android "App Links" so invite links opened the app instead of falling back to a browser.
- **Ludo color/seating bugs** (reported via screenshot): fixed the stale-position cache causing the color mismatch; then, after a follow-up report that the fix over-corrected two-player seating, rebuilt board placement to be a function of join order rather than chosen color, guaranteeing correct opposite seating regardless of which colors players pick.
- **Scramble dictionary bugs**: removed an artificial cap on the bundled word list (192k+ words vs. the old 28k), added cross-match word-repeat prevention, and fixed the Firestore nested-array bug that was silently breaking new multiplayer matches.
- **Scramble feature-parity request** ("In scramble multi-player game leadership board is missing," "play screen should be fixed...," "provide option for quick rematch," "provide all features and setup like single player game," "provide option to revisit passed words," "provide meaning and usage for solved words") — all six were implemented directly from these tester reports: added a leaderboard link, fixed the scrollable layout, added shuffle/pass/hint parity with single-player, added a "revisit passed words" mechanic, added a rematch flow, and added post-solve word definitions with example sentences (single- and multiplayer).
- **Feedback/admin UI**: removed the forced-download behavior on screenshot taps; fixed inaccurate usage stats on the About screen; added push notifications so users are notified when their own feedback is replied to or resolved (previously silent).

### 8. Who is the target audience of your app?

Families and close friend groups who regularly share expenses — rent, groceries, trips, recurring household bills — and want a single place to split and track them fairly, alongside light social features (in-app multiplayer games and chat) that give the group a reason to open the app together, not just when money changes hands.

### 9. How does your app provide value to users?

It removes the friction and awkwardness of manually tracking who paid for what in a shared household or friend group: automatic equal/percentage/exact-amount splitting, recurring-bill reminders so nothing gets forgotten, budgets and spend alerts, and a shared activity feed so everyone in the group can see what's happening without asking. The built-in games and chat are a secondary hook that keeps the same family/friend group coming back to the app between expense events.

### 10. How many installs do you expect after launch?

A modest, realistic estimate: **50–200 installs in the first 3 months**. This is based on real current usage — 55 real testers already using the app, drawn almost entirely from a personal family/friend network — plus a small amount of organic growth expected from a new in-app feature that lets happy users share a personalized usage recap to WhatsApp/Facebook. This is not a mass-market consumer app; growth is expected to stay close to word-of-mouth within similar family/friend circles rather than broad public acquisition.

## Alternate 3-question version

**What did you learn from closed testing?**
That the core expense-splitting flow worked well, but several supporting features (activity feed, chat, one specific game mode) had real usability gaps testers hit repeatedly — sorting/filtering, seating/color logic in a board game, and a scramble word game missing feature parity between its single- and multiplayer modes. Structured in-app feedback (vs. relying on informal chat) surfaced specific, actionable reports rather than vague impressions.

**How did you gather feedback?**
An in-app Feedback screen (suggestion/feedback/bug-tagged submissions with optional screenshots, reviewed via an admin dashboard) as the primary channel, supplemented by informal check-ins in the WhatsApp group used to recruit testers.

**What changes did you make based on testing?**
Fixed multiple reported bugs (chat/activity sorting, app-lock re-entry, expense display, Ludo board-seating logic, Scramble dictionary/duplicate-word bugs, a Firestore data bug breaking new matches) and shipped a full round of tester-requested features (Scramble leaderboard, layout fix, rematch, hint/shuffle/pass parity, word definitions, passed-word revisits), each traceable to a specific tester-submitted report.
